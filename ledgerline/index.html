Why two return numbers. Money-weighted return (IRR) answers “how did I do”, and is dragged around by when cash went in. Time-weighted return strips the timing of contributions out, so it measures the strategy and is the only one comparable to an index. Both are computed here from the same daily series and the same flow ledger, never typed in.

Scores are computed, not asserted. Each factor is a percentile rank within the equity universe. Value blends the P/E and P/B ranks, quality blends return on invested capital, gross margin, accruals and leverage, growth blends earnings growth and margin trend, while momentum (12-1 month) and low-volatility come from each holding's own price history. Ranks are blended, never raw values, since averaging a P/E with a margin is meaningless. The fundamental inputs are synthetic; the ranking and blending logic is real and lives in buildFactors(). ETFs, crypto and cash have no comparable income statement, so they are excluded from the ranking rather than scored on partial data.

Drift is not a reason to trade on its own. Selling to correct an overweight realises gains and costs spread, so the policy here is a tolerance band: nothing is actioned until a class sits more than five percentage points from its target. Where drift can be corrected with new contributions instead of sales, that route is costed separately below, because it fixes the weights without a taxable event.

About this project. Ledgerline is a personal build: a single-page analytics layer over a synthetic multi-currency book. There is no backend and no market data provider. Every price, holding and cash flow is invented, generated from a seeded model in the browser at page load.

What is real is the analytics on top. Time-weighted return with irregular cash flows, the money-weighted IRR solver, volatility, Sharpe, beta, drawdown, capture ratios, the correlation matrix, concentration measures, the scenario engine and the factor ranking all compute from those generated series. Nothing on these six tabs is a number I typed twice, which is why the tiles, the tables and the charts agree with each other. Point the same code at a real price feed and this layer works unchanged. The parts I cared about were the ones that are easy to get quietly wrong: not annualising a three-week window, keeping an asset move separate from a currency move, comparing IRR and TWRR over the same period, and blending factor ranks rather than raw values that share no units.
