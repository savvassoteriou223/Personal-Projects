# How to Make a Website for Helix (total beginner guide)

You don't need to know any coding or design. This is like following a recipe.
Set aside an afternoon. Read it once, then do it step by step.

---

## First, what are we even making, and why?

A "landing page" is just **one simple web page** — one screen you scroll down —
that tells people what Helix is and gives them a button to get it. That's it.
Not a big website with menus. One page.

Why you need it: on Instagram and TikTok, you're only allowed to put **one link**
on your profile. That one link has to go somewhere. It goes to this page. The
page then shows people your app and sends them to download it. It also makes you
look real and trustworthy when someone looks you up.

We'll build it using a website called **Carrd** (spelled with two r's, at
carrd.co). Carrd lets you build a page by clicking and typing — no code. Think of
it like making a poster in PowerPoint, but it becomes a web page.

One thing about timing: **your app isn't public on Google Play yet** (Google is
still reviewing it). So for now, instead of a "Download" button that goes
nowhere, we'll put an **email sign-up box** ("Get notified when Helix launches").
When the app goes live, you'll swap in the real download button. I'll show you
both.

## STEP 1 — Make a Carrd account (5 minutes)

1. Open your web browser and go to **carrd.co**
2. Click the button that says **"Sign up"** (top right corner).
3. Sign up with your Google account or an email + password. Easy.

You now have an account. That's all Step 1 is.

---

## STEP 2 — Pick a starting design (5 minutes)

1. After signing up, Carrd shows you a wall of **templates** (ready-made designs).
2. Look for a category called **"Sections"** and click it. These are the ones
   built for scrolling pages like ours.
3. Pick any one you think looks clean. A dark/black one fits Helix's style. Don't
   overthink it — you're going to replace all the words and pictures anyway. The
   template is just a skeleton.
4. Click it. It opens the **editor** — the screen where you build your page.

**How the editor works (read this slowly):**
- The middle of the screen shows your page.
- If you **click on anything** — a word, a picture, a button — a menu opens on the
  right side where you can change it.
- There's a **"+" button** near the top-left. That's how you ADD new things (more
  text, a picture, a button, etc.).
- Top-right there's a little **rocket icon** — that's the "publish" (go live)
  button. We'll use it at the end. And an **eye icon** to preview.

Don't worry about breaking anything. Nothing is public until you hit the rocket.

---

## STEP 3 — Write your headline (10 minutes)

1. Click on the big title text at the top of the page.
2. A box opens on the right. Delete what's there and type: **Helix**
3. Click the smaller text under it (the subtitle). Type something like:
   **Your AI coach. Snap a photo to log your meals, and train around your
   recovery.**

That's your headline done. This is the first thing people read, so it just needs
to say what the app is in one line.

---

## STEP 4 — Add a video of the app (15 minutes)

A short clip of the food-photo feature is the most convincing thing on the page.
Here's the easy way to add one.

**Option A — the simplest (a looping mini-clip, called a "GIF"):**
1. Record your phone screen using the food-scan feature (Android has a screen
   recorder in the swipe-down menu). Keep it under 10 seconds.
2. On your computer, go to **ezgif.com**, click **"Video to GIF"**, upload your
   clip, and click convert. A GIF is just a short silent video that plays on a
   loop by itself. Download it.
3. Back in Carrd: click the **"+"** button → choose **Image** → upload your GIF.
   It will play automatically on your page. Done.

**Option B — a normal video (if you'd rather):**
1. Upload your clip to **YouTube** and set it to **"Unlisted"** (that means only
   people with the link can see it — it won't show up publicly). Copy its link.
2. In Carrd: click **"+"** → choose **Embed** → paste the YouTube link.

Either works. Option A looks cleaner. Pick one.

---

## STEP 5 — Add three "feature" sections (20 minutes)

These are three little blocks, each with a picture and a couple of sentences,
explaining the three best things your app does.

1. Most templates already have picture-and-text blocks built in. Click a picture
   → on the right, choose to **replace** it → upload a screenshot of your app.
   (The same screenshots you made for the Play Store are perfect.)
2. Click the text next to it and write two short sentences. Use these:

   - **Photo food logging:** "Take a photo of your plate. Helix estimates your
     calories and macros in seconds — no searching, no typing."
   - **AI coach:** "A coach that knows your goals, your equipment, and your
     history — and adjusts your plan whenever you ask."
   - **Recovery:** "Helix looks at your sleep and heart rate to spot low-energy
     days and tell you how to train around them."

3. If there are too many blocks, click one and press the **Delete** key. If you
   need another, click a block and copy-paste it (Ctrl+C then Ctrl+V on Windows).

---

## STEP 6 — Add the sign-up box (10 minutes)

Because the app isn't downloadable yet, we collect emails so you can tell people
the moment it launches.

1. Click **"+"** → choose **Form**. (A "form" is just a box where visitors type
   their email and click a button.)
2. Click the form → on the right, change the button to say **"Get early access"**
   and the heading to **"Be the first to know when Helix launches."**
3. In the form's settings on the right, set it to send the emails to
   **fittechdevs@gmail.com** so you receive every sign-up.

**Later, when the app is live**, you'll come back and add a real download button:
- Click **"+"** → **Image** → upload the official "Get it on Google Play" picture
  (download it from google's badge page).
- Click that picture → on the right, find **Link** → paste this exact address:
  `https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.helixfit.app`
- That makes the picture clickable and sends people to your app.

---

## STEP 7 — Add the small print at the bottom (10 minutes)

Google requires you to have a Privacy Policy and Terms. You already wrote these
for the Play Store, so we just reuse them.

1. Scroll to the bottom of your page. Click **"+"** → **Text**.
2. Type your support email: **fittechdevs@gmail.com**
3. For the Privacy Policy and Terms: in Carrd you can add extra pages (look for a
   small pages/layers menu at the top-left). Add two pages, paste your existing
   Privacy and Terms text into them, and link the bottom of your main page to
   them. If this part confuses you, skip it for now and just leave your email —
   you can add it before you start advertising.

---

## STEP 8 — Put it live (10 minutes)

1. Click the **rocket icon** at the top-right. This means "publish" = make it
   visible on the internet.
2. Carrd asks for a name. Type something like **helixfit**. Your page is now live
   at **helixfit.carrd.co** — a real web address you can share. 
3. That's it. You have a live website.

**(Optional, later) Getting a nicer web address like helixfit.app:**
A "domain" is just a custom web address (helixfit.app instead of
helixfit.carrd.co). It looks more professional. To get one:
1. Go to **cloudflare.com**, make a free account, and under "Register Domains"
   buy `helixfit.app` (about $12/year).
2. In Carrd, upgrade to "Pro Standard" ($19/year), then in the publish screen type
   your new domain. Carrd will show you a couple of settings to copy.
3. Copy those settings into Cloudflare (under "DNS"). This step tells the internet
   "helixfit.app should show my Carrd page." It can take a few hours to start
   working — that's normal, not broken.

If that last part sounds intimidating, ignore it for now. The free
helixfit.carrd.co address works perfectly to get started.

---

## STEP 9 — Check it works (5 minutes)

1. Open your new web address **on your phone** — that's where almost everyone will
   see it. Make sure the video plays and the words fit the screen.
2. Type your own email into the sign-up box and submit it, to confirm the emails
   actually reach you.
